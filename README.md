# Exploration-of-AI-Use-Case-Case-study-on-self-driving-cars
# AIM
To study the application of Artificial Intelligence (AI) in self-driving cars and understand how AI technologies help vehicles perceive their surroundings, make decisions, and navigate safely without continuous human control.
# THEORY
Artificial Intelligence (AI) enables machines to perform tasks that usually require human intelligence, such as learning, recognizing objects, making decisions, and solving problems.

A self-driving car, also known as an autonomous vehicle, is a vehicle that uses AI, sensors, cameras, and computing systems to understand its surroundings and perform driving tasks with varying levels of automation.

Self-driving cars combine information from their environment to identify roads, vehicles, pedestrians, traffic signs, and obstacles. Based on this information, the AI system determines an appropriate driving action, such as slowing down, stopping, changing lanes, or following a route.

Examples of companies developing autonomous driving technology include Waymo, Tesla, and Mercedes-Benz. Their systems differ in their sensors, capabilities, and levels of automation.
# WORKING PRINCIPLE
Self-driving cars operate through a continuous cycle of sensing, perception, decision-making, planning, and control.
1. Environment Sensing: Cameras and other sensors collect information about roads, traffic signals, pedestrians, vehicles, and nearby obstacles.
2. Object Detection: AI models analyse sensor data to identify and classify objects such as cars, cyclists, traffic signs, and pedestrians.
3. Sensor Fusion: Information from different sensors is combined to obtain a more reliable understanding of the vehicle's surroundings.
4. Localization: GPS, maps, and other sensor measurements help estimate the vehicle's position and direction.
5. Prediction: AI systems estimate how nearby vehicles, pedestrians, and other road users may move.
6. Decision Making: The system selects suitable driving actions, such as slowing down, stopping, or yielding to pedestrians.
7. Path Planning: The system determines a safe path and appropriate speed while considering road conditions and traffic rules.
8. Vehicle Control: Electronic controllers send commands to the steering, acceleration, and braking systems.
9. Feedback: Sensors continuously monitor the surroundings and vehicle movement, allowing the system to update its actions when conditions change.
# ADVANTAGES
- Can reduce some types of human driving errors.
- Provides continuous environmental monitoring.
- Can improve traffic flow when properly integrated.
- Supports automated navigation and parking.
- May improve mobility for certain users.
- Can increase efficiency in controlled transport environments.
- Reduces the need for continuous manual input in supported automated-driving conditions.
# LIMITATIONS
- Performance may be affected by heavy rain, fog, glare, or poor visibility.
- Complex road situations can be difficult to interpret.
- Sensor failures or incorrect detections can affect driving decisions.
- Advanced hardware and software can be expensive.
- Cybersecurity and data privacy are important concerns.
- Legal requirements and infrastructure vary between locations.
- Fully autonomous operation is not available in every vehicle or environment.
- Some automated-driving systems require a human driver to remain attentive and ready to take control.
# RESULT
The application of Artificial Intelligence in self-driving cars was studied successfully. The roles of computer vision, Machine Learning, sensor fusion, prediction, path planning, and vehicle-control systems were understood. The working principle, components, applications, advantages, and limitations of autonomous vehicles were examined through the Waymo case study.
